    #66 The first paint holds still on every arrival

    from #8real use #71mock-green

    Spec of #8, grilled 2026-10-17 (/grill-with-docs #8). It folds in #57 (the first paint owns its covers end to end) and #13 (an unknown link's first paint).

    Spec of #8, grilled 2026-10-17 (/grill-with-docs #8). It folds in #57 (the first paint owns its covers end to end) and #13 (an unknown link's first paint).

    Problem Statement

    A reader who comes back to the interview, or arrives from a country or route page, sees the page move under them before they have done anything.

    • The footer jumps. The build paints question one in the box. Under a cover, the box shows the reader's stand-in line. When the module lands, about 700 ms later on a phone, it draws the reader's own screen, which is not the height of the built one. Everything below moves. Measured on master 0b1b070 with the module held 700 ms:
    • a country link: +249 px at 390x1400;
    • a route link: +246 px;
    • a saved record: +44 px. At 1280x800 the footer is in view, so this is a visible shift on a laptop too.
    • The masthead grows under an unknown link. A link whose code the page does not know (?country=zz) is painted with the short standing promise, the one for a reader who has started. The module then finds nothing answered and writes the long one. The masthead grows 94 px and takes the whole interview with it (CLS 0.0565 at 390x844).
    • The ledger's count widens. It goes from "nothing yet" to the reader's count in view (0.0026).

    The field reads the same thing. Cloudflare metric 6 read 88 % good on 2026-09-23 against a target above 92 %.

    Behind it is a shape that has already made one bug. The first paint's covers are written by the head script, hidden and shown by the page's stylesheet, and taken off in three places in the page's script. One attribute carries two meanings: the cover, and the masthead's standing promise. Taking a cover off is written as "set the promise to started". A record cover left standing once drew a question card with every child invisible (s20's walk). The stylesheet's narrow/wide threshold is a hand copy of the script's.

    Solution

    The first paint holds still for every reader, on every arrival, at every width the product is measured at:

    • A reader who comes back, or arrives from a link, sees the frame and their stand-in line. The footer and the ledger's count take no room, or stay unseen, until the module draws their screen. Then everything appears where it stays.
    • The masthead is always the height of its longer standing promise, so whichever promise the page settles on, nothing below it moves.
    • A fresh visit and a reader owed a verdict see exactly what they see today.

    One module owns the first paint end to end:

    • the head script that writes the covers;
    • the stylesheet rules keyed to them, with the narrow/wide threshold written once;
    • the stand-ins they uncover;
    • a single covers off step the page calls once, in the task that draws the reader's first screen.

    The masthead's standing promise is the page's own state and no longer shares an attribute with a cover.

    User Stories

    1. As a reader returning to an unfinished interview on a phone, I want the page to stay still while my answers come back, so that I am not reading a line that jumps away.
    2. As a reader returning to an unfinished interview on a tall phone (390x1400), I want the footer not to slide down when my question appears, so that the page does not look broken.
    3. As a reader on a laptop (1280x800) returning with a saved record, I want the footer, which I can see, to stay where it is, so that the first thing I see does not lurch.
    4. As a reader who pressed "Check yours" on a country page, I want the page to hold still until my questions are set up, so that I trust what I am looking at.
    5. As a reader who pressed "Check yours" on a route page, I want the same stillness, so that a route arrival is no worse than a country arrival.
    6. As a reader who followed a link with a country or route code the site no longer knows, I want the masthead not to grow under me when the page discovers that, so that a stale link costs me nothing.
    7. As a reader arriving from any link or with a record, I want the stand-in line to be the only thing in the box until my screen is drawn, so that nothing untrue is ever on screen.
    8. As a reader arriving with a record or a link, I want the footer to appear only when my screen is drawn, and where it stays, so that it never moves once seen.
    9. As a reader arriving with a record or a link, I want the ledger's count to appear only when it shows my count, so that I never see "nothing yet" turn into a number.
    10. As a fresh visitor, I want the page to paint once and never change, as it does today, so that the fix for others costs me nothing.
    11. As a fresh visitor, I want the masthead's promise to read as it does today, with at most the space of the longer promise beneath it, so that the page still says what it is.
    12. As a reader who has started, I want the short promise in a masthead that does not resize, so that the page does not jump when I come back.
    13. As a reader owed a verdict, I want the hold state exactly as today (the frame and the hold line, nothing else laid out), so that my return still lands where it stays.
    14. As a reader with JavaScript disabled, I want the page the build painted, unchanged and uncovered, so that nothing is hidden from me by a script that never ran.
    15. As a reader whose browser refuses site data, I want to be treated as a fresh visitor or a link arrival, as today, so that a blocked store never leaves a cover standing.
    16. As a reader who later corrects an answer, starts over, or moves through history, I want no cover from the first paint still standing, so that no card is ever drawn with its children invisible.
    17. As a reader on a narrow screen, I want the ledger folded away and its line above the question from the first paint, as today, so that the ledger never moves after the module lands.
    18. As a reader on a wide screen, I want the ledger to be a panel from the first paint, as today.
    19. As the maintainer, I want every cover the head script can write to have a rule that hides it and a single step that takes it off, provable without a browser, so that s20's bug cannot come back.
    20. As the maintainer, I want the narrow/wide threshold of the first paint's rules to be written once, so that the script and the stylesheet cannot disagree.
    21. As the maintainer, I want the cover and the standing promise to be separate things in the page, so that taking a cover off never rewrites a promise and writing a promise never re-covers anything.

    From the grill of #8 (2026-10-17); each was the human's ruling.

    1. One slice, in order. #57's module comes first and is a move: behaviour unchanged, the measurements read as before. #13's and #8's fixes land on it afterwards. Two sequential tickets. They touch the same files, so they are not built in parallel.

    Spec for #14, "A flag quotes a page; the quote is the page's". It folds in #54, "What a watch run says, out of the CLI". Repository: OytunOnal/permit-rulebook-data (the watch). Bump: Z.

    Problem Statement

    A curator walks a flag by reading the issue the watch opened. In that issue, the page's words around the change are printed as a Markdown blockquote. Markdown still acts inside a blockquote, so the page can act on our tracker:

    • an @login in a source's text notifies a stranger;
    • a #12 leaves a cross-reference on one of our issues;
    • a [text](url) becomes a link, and emphasis restyles what the curator reads;
    • an &lt;img src=…&gt; in the page survives tag stripping, because entities are decoded after tags are removed, and renders as an image.

    Two kinds of character also pass the text normalizer: bidi overrides and isolates, and control bytes such as ESC. A bidi override reorders what follows it in the issue. An ESC starts an escape sequence in the terminal of a curator who prints the flag file.

    Our own words run into the page's. The ellipsis marks around the excerpt are ours, but they sit inside the page's text. The flag's sections are joined without blank lines, so the remediation sentence renders as part of the page's quotation: watch/flags/nl-ind-blue-card-2026-10-16.md shows it.

    The excerpt is cut by UTF-16 units. A cut can fall between the two halves of an astral character, and the flag then shows a replacement character the page never wrote.

    Sanitising the page's words would change what the curator reads. This is why the fix is the flag's format, not its content.

    The code that writes a flag cannot be tested, which is how all of this went unseen. The watch's CLI does its decision work at module top level and ends in process.exit. That work is the flag's body and name, the remediation, the log level per outcome, the --only filter, the read_at line and the BOM-tolerant read. The tests can only check flags already committed, not the code that writes them (#54).

    Solution

    A flag's excerpt is set apart so that nothing in it acts on the page it is shown in, and every word around it is ours:

    • The excerpt sits in a fenced code block that its own text cannot close.
    • Characters that steer how text is drawn are dropped, and control bytes become a space. This is the rule the watch already applies to every page-written fragment it prints.
    • No other character of the page's words is changed.
    • Our sentence introducing the excerpt stands outside the block, and blank lines separate each section of the flag.
    • The excerpt is cut on characters, never inside one.

    What a finished run says moves into a module of its own: its flags, its log events and its exit code, returned as data. The CLI only reads files, writes them and exits. The flag's format is then asserted by tests that call that module. One of those tests renders the flag with a real Markdown renderer.

    Implementation Decisions

    Excerpt is the glossary's word (#14, now in GLOSSARY.md): the page's own words around the first difference, as the run read them. It is cut at both ends, carries nothing that steers how text is drawn, and is otherwise never rewritten. A quote stays the dataset's. The report's field for it is renamed from context to excerpt. No log line or committed file carries that name today.

    The excerpt's block.

    • The excerpt is written in a fenced code block with the info string text.
    • The fence is made of backticks, one longer than the longest run of backticks in the excerpt, and never shorter than three. The page's text cannot close it.
    • The excerpt is one line, because the normalizer has already collapsed the page's whitespace.

    What is taken out of the excerpt.

    • It passes the watch's existing printable rule, which lives in the failure module: every Default_Ignorable_Code_Point is dropped, and runs of whitespace and control bytes (C0, DEL, C1) collapse to one space.
    • No length bound is added, because the cut already bounds it.
    • The rule stays in one place. The flag asks for it; nobody writes a second copy.
    • Nothing else changes: words, punctuation and Markdown characters are left as the page wrote them.

    What stands outside the block.

    • The ellipsis marks leave the excerpt.
    • A line of ours stands directly above the fence: "The page now reads, around the first difference (cut at both ends):".

    An empty excerpt.

    • If the excerpt prints nothing once the rule has run, there is no introducing line and no fence.
    • One line of ours stands in their place: "The page's words around the first difference print nothing; read the page."
    • A report with no excerpt (a reminder, a pdf bytes change) writes neither, as today.

    The flag's sections.

    • The title line and the field lines (url, kind, date, old, new) are one block with no blank line inside it. The committed-flags test reads the header as everything up to the first blank line.
    • After that block, every section is separated by one blank line: the intent, the excerpt (or its empty line), and the remediation.
    • Field values stay as today: the url through the printable address rule, and the intent as the watchlist's own words.

    The cut.

    • The excerpt is cut by Unicode code points, not UTF-16 units. The first difference and both ends of the span are found in characters.
    • The span stays as it is: 60 characters before the first difference and 120 from it.

    The module, "what a run says". A new watch module turns a finished run into what it says, as data:

    • The run. Input: the reports, the verdict, today, and what became of the state, as data rather than a flag: either "committed", with the entry count, the targeted id (if any) and last_run, or "dry run". Output:
    • the flag documents, each a name and a body. A reminder takes <id>-reminder.md and a change <id>-<date>.md. Only changed and reminder-due produce one.
    • the log events, each a level, a message and its fields: one per report, at its level per outcome; the unread-source notices; the state committed or dry-run line; and watch complete with the run summary.
    • the exit code: 1 when the verdict is red, 0 otherwise.
    • The remediation moves with the flag: a sentinel's own sentence, except on a due reminder, and otherwise the strategy's sentence from the strategy table.
    • The `--only` choice. Given the watchlist and the argument, it returns the entries to run, or "no such watch entry" with exit code 2.
    • The `read_at` decision. Given the entry and a fetch result, it returns a read_at event when an ok reading was taken at another address, and nothing otherwise.
    • The BOM-tolerant parse takes text and returns the parsed object. Reading the file stays in the CLI.
    • What stays in the CLI: reading the watchlist and state files, opening and closing Chrome and logging each browser read's cost, running the watch, writing flag files and the state file, printing log lines, and exiting.
    • Ticket 1 changes nothing. Moving the decisions changes no byte of any flag, log message or exit code.

    The order. Two tickets, the second blocked by the first:

    1. What a run says, out of the CLI (#54). This ticket changes no behaviour.
    2. The flag's excerpt is the page's (#14). It brings the block, the printable rule, the line of ours, the sections, the empty excerpt and the code-point cut.

    Solution

    One deep module owns the trust line. It answers four questions, one for each moment a source has:

    • the entry address;
    • a redirect target;
    • the addresses a name resolves to;
    • the page a reader ended on.

    Each answer is a refusal, which names the kind of address and never the address, or nothing. Beside the four questions sit the printers, which show an address the way a person should see it.     Implementation Decisions

    Numbered as the grill settled them (#65's decisions comment), so tickets can carry them.

    1. One module decides every kept issue. overdue flags, watch run failed, deploy run failed and check failed on master all go through one table: what the run found × the issue's state → the action. Each issue's own words (title, label, body) stay with the run that keeps it.
    2. The module lives in the data package, exported as an entry of its own beside the watch's run module. The deploy's report reads it from the data commit data.lock pins: that commit has built and deployed once, so a broken data master cannot silence the report. The data half lands first; the site half follows once a deploy has pinned a data commit that holds the module.
    3. Which deploy runs keep the issue: only schedule and repository_dispatch. A push and a workflow_dispatch have a person at the other end.
    4. Which reds count: any of build, deploy, pin, audit. IndexNow is continue-on-error and never reddens the run. The body names the red jobs and says in one line whether today's page was published.
    5. Which green closes it: only a green schedule or repository_dispatch run, with a closing line linking it. A push builds against the pinned data and proves nothing about the daily page.
    6. The deploy's issue: title deploy run failed, under a new red deploy label ("The daily deploy itself"), created by the run when missing.
    7. Where it lives: each run's kept issue lives in its own repository's tracker. No cross-repository token.
    8. The data check's triggers: push to master, pull_request, and workflow_run after source-watch completes (any conclusion). Each checks out master's head, or the pull request's head.
    9. The data check's kept issue: only workflow_run reds and greens keep check failed on master, under a new check label ("The data repository's own suite"). Its body links the run and names the master commit it checked.
    10. `watch run failed` closes itself on a non-measurement run where every step but the overdue clock passed, with a line linking it. A measurement run neither opens nor closes it.
    11. The seam. The glossary's Kept issue. The module's interface, settled in the grill:
        /** What the run found, in the issue's terms. */
    type RunFinding =
      | { kind: "red"; body: string }        // comment, or create, or reopen + comment
      | { kind: "green"; closing: string }   // close an open issue with this line
      | { kind: "unsaid" };                  // leave the issue as it is (the clock unread)

    /** The issue a run keeps, as written in its workflow. */
    interface KeptIssue { title: string; label: string }

    type KeptIssueAction =
      | { kind: "create"; title: string; label: string; body: string }
      | { kind: "comment"; number: number; reopen: boolean; body: string }
      | { kind: "close"; number: number; comment: string }
      | { kind: "nothing" }
      | { kind: "unread"; reason: string };

    /** `listed` is the tracker's answer as text (number, state and title of each issue under the label). */
    function actionOnKeptIssue(issue: KeptIssue, finding: RunFinding, listed: string): KeptIssueAction

    The issue is found by its exact title, open or closed. unread is the run's step failing, never a silent "nothing".

    1. Who runs `gh`: one CLI reads the tracker, decides through actionOnKeptIssue, turns the action into exact gh argument lists through a pure function (ghArgumentsOf(action)), and runs them with no shell. The workflows hand it the issue and the finding.
    2. gh is found from PATH's absolute folders only, never from the folder under check (Security: an outside program).
    3. The tracker read asks for a fixed limit, and an answer at or over it is unread; the flags list is bounded the same way (Security: every loop over outside input is bounded).
    4. The deploy's `report` job: the last job of the deploy workflow. It needs build, deploy, pin and audit, runs whatever their result on schedule and repository_dispatch only, and is the only job holding issues: write. A site module, deployFindingOf(jobResults, runUrl), turns the jobs' results into the finding and its words; the job hands that finding to the data package's CLI built from the data.lock commit.
    5. Job results the module cannot read (malformed, a job missing, an unknown result) give a red finding that says the report could not read the jobs' results, never green (Security: data crossing a boundary is validated).
    6. Flag issues: FlagDocument carries its title. The watch's CLI writes this run's new flags (file and title) to a list outside the repository, in the runner's temporary directory. After the persist step has pushed, one CLI files one source-change issue per flag on that list through the same adapter, the body read from the flag file. The git status, head, sed and path-guard bash goes.
    7. One printer, one command per tracker step. One print(LogEvent) in a small file of its own, used by every CLI; the two copies go. Every step that touches a tracker is one CLI command, the clock's read of the open flags included: the clock's CLI reads through the adapter, turns readClock's reading into a finding (findingOfClock), keeps overdue flags, and still exits red on overdue.
    8. The data check workflow: a check job with contents: read only, on ubuntu-26.04 (named, so the browser cases drive the image's own Chrome), running npm ci and npm run check; a report job after it with issues: write, on workflow_run only. No pull_request_target. The actions and their full SHAs are the watch's.
    9. The bump: Z, bug.
    10. Real use: (1) the first scheduled morning after the data half's merge: the check runs by workflow_run and is green, and data#31 closes itself if every source reads, or gains a comment and stays if an outage holds; (2) after the site half's merge, on the human's word at that moment, one repository_dispatch carrying a sha that is not a data commit opens deploy run failed, and the next 06:40 scheduled deploy closes it; (3) the human says whether that issue reached their own device before the session pointed at it, and whether its body told them which job went red and whether the page was published. The check's red path is proven before the PR, not in real use: a red data master on purpose would redden the live deploy.
    11. Out of scope: see below.

    Testing Decisions

    A good test asserts a decision through a public interface: the action for a finding and a tracker state, the argument lists for an action, the finding for a set of job results. It never asserts the wording of a body beyond what the decision requires (which jobs are named, whether "published" is said, that the run is linked).

    The seams, agreed before the spec:

    Data repository

    1. actionOnKeptIssue: every cell of findings (red, green, unsaid) × the tracker's answer (no such issue, open, closed, unreadable), the exact-title match against a near-miss title, and an unreadable answer giving unread.
    2. ghArgumentsOf: reopen before comment, the label created before an issue is created, a title or body passed as one argument.
    3. findingOfClock: clear → green, overdue → red with its body, unread → unsaid.
    4. sayRun (existing): every FlagDocument carries its title.
    5. The kept-issue CLI and the flag-filing CLI as child processes, with a stub gh first on PATH that answers the tracker read and records every argument list: one case per action, and a hostile title ($(…), quotes, a backtick) that reaches gh intact. This is where the red path is proven before the PR. Prior art: the site's carry tests' entryRunAsChildProcess.
    6. The workflow YAML tests (existing workflow-text helpers, rebuild-dispatch.test.ts): step order, each tracker step's if: (measurement runs keep nothing; check failed on master only on workflow_run), and each job's permissions. Nothing about a step's script.

    Site repository

    1. deployFindingOf: each red job named, published or not by the deploy job's result, green only when all four passed or were skipped by design, the run linked.
    2. tests/pipeline.test.ts (existing): report needs the four jobs, runs whatever their result on schedule and repository_dispatch only, and is the only job with issues: write.

    The gh adapter inside the CLI is covered by seam 5 only.

    Problem Statement

    A reader meets the dataset's words where they expect sentences:

    • Headings and counts speak the model's language: "the Netherlands: 6 routes scored, 1 quoted.", "quoted and dated · scored — 2 conditions this interview did not ask", "Your 9 answers were compared against 12 published rule sets.", and "scored against your answers" on pages where they have answered nothing.

Shorthand is explained on first use on each page, through the one gloss the route page already uses. Two recognition situations get two options. A link to a page that is not the authority's says what it is. A question that names a route says what the route is. A territory typed into the passport search is answered with the passport its people hold.

    Implementation Decisions

    Words (rulings 2–5, 17).

    • The reader's verb is "checked"; "scored", "rule set(s)" and "this interview" leave every reader screen but /data/. "Condition" stays the reader's word.
    • The scope line has two forms, chosen by whether the reader has answered. Results card: "quoted and dated · checked against your answers" · "quoted and dated · checked against your answers, except N conditions we don't ask about" · "quoted and dated · not checked against your answers". Country and route pages: "quoted and dated · the checker asks about all of it" · "… the checker asks about it, except N conditions" · "… the checker does not ask about it". Where unasked limbs are Route readings, both forms end "— M of them in our own words, not the authority's".
    • The data package's scope-line function takes which form it is asked for; the site picks the form by page. The site's own sentences that carried "scored" ("Quoted here, not scored", "Not scored for X yet", "No scored route in …", "is quoted here but not scored", "the checker does not score", "we state and do not score", "the ones quoted and not scored") take "checked" or "asks" by the same rule; their words stay in the site's copy module.
    • Country h1: "<Country>: N routes. The checker asks about M." The social card and the home page's description count the same way ("29 routes · the checker asks about 23").
    • Results masthead: "Your N answers were checked against the published rules of M routes."
    • Dataset text in the data repository: the six quoted-only reasons' "This route is not scored" becomes "This route is not checked against your answers"; the two "this interview" phrases become "we don't ask"; summaries that say "Quoted here, not scored" follow.
    • /data/ keeps its terms: its lede, its Routes row, its gate lines, its meta description and its JSON-LD description.
    • GLOSSARY's Scope statement already carries both forms and the avoid-list (committed with The Road before the build).

    A country's name in a sentence (ruling 6).

    • One data-package function returns a country's phrase with its article from the country list, in three shapes: within a sentence ("the Netherlands"), at a sentence or heading start ("The Netherlands"), and possessive ("the Netherlands'"). It throws on a country it does not know.
    • Every reader place that prints a country's name in running text uses it: the country h1, the interview's arrival line, the route page's description, the verdict lines, the unscored-route lines. The site's own article set and possessive helper are deleted.
    • The country list gives an article to every name that takes one in English running text (today only the Netherlands carries one; the United States, the United Kingdom, the Philippines, the Gambia, the Bahamas, the Maldives, the United Arab Emirates, the Dominican Republic, the Central African Republic and the other "Republic"/"Islands"/plural names need it). The phrase is only as right as that list.
    • Labels that are a country's name alone (a results section heading, a chip, a list row, /data/'s row label) keep the bare name.

    Place slots (ruling 7).

    • The two situation phrases with a place slot are rewritten to read with any place: "An employment contract directly with an employer {place} — including one you already hold — not a transfer to a branch on a contract you hold abroad." and "Your contract stays with the company abroad and it moves you to its branch {place}."
    • The filler gives "in <the country's phrase>" when a place is known and "in the country you move to" when none is; "there" is never a fill.

    Criteria with paths (rulings 12–13).

    • A criterion with paths is named, in every one-line form (the route card's heading, the results' verdict line), by its own label, capitalised at a heading start: "Recognised degree or IT experience".
    • The route card's body for it is "Either path meets it:" and one line per path, "<path label>: <the path's criteria joined with 'and'>". The words are the site's copy; the labels are the dataset's.
    • The IT path's experience criterion gets the short_reason "at least 3 years of related experience in the last seven".
    • An enum question may be marked ordered in the dataset: its options run from least to most of one amount. Today that is the two experience questions; a contributor marks any other ladder the same way.

    Shorthand (ruling 8).

    Problem Statement

    A reader with an Indian passport and a German job offer answers "And in the last seven years?" with "3 to under 5 years", and no Germany card reads it: "Under 3" and "3 to under 5" give the same result on all eight German cards. The German Blue Card's own source, the ZAV page the dataset already quotes and the watch reads daily, has a special rule for IT specialists: no formal qualification, three years of relevant IT experience in the last seven, €45,934.20. Nothing models it, so an IT worker without a degree is never shown the Blue Card the law gives them, and the card's scope line ("every rule that decides … is a question you answer") is untrue.

    Around it, the interview asks questions without saying why, and keeps or drops answers without saying so:
