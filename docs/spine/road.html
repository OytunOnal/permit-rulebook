    #6 [new-need] The results screen has no door: ask for feedback at the moment the judgement happensclosed

    Reported by a reader through the maker (2026-09-10): the product should ask for feedback at some point — as a prompt that opens, or as an icon that is always there.

    Reported by a reader through the maker (2026-09-10): the product should ask for feedback at some point — as a prompt that opens, or as an icon that is always there.

    Where it stands today. The footer of every page carries "Report a wrong value" and "Suggest a route or a change", and a route page repeats the first one in its body, beside the data. The results screen — where a reader actually decides the product is wrong about them — carries neither in its body. So the moment the judgement happens is the moment with no door.

    What was tried and rejected. Two shapes were drawn in docs/spine/design/s7-feedback.html and the maker rejected both (2026-09-10):

    • A, a slip docked to the bottom edge — appears once after the results have been read, never dims the page, closable.
    • B, a centred modal over a dimmed page — the same words, but it stops the reading.

    Neither is the answer. Whatever replaces them has to keep the rules both were written under, because they are what make this product what it is:

    • it never appears before the reader has an answer, and never during the interview;
    • once, ever, remembered on the device;
    • it asks only for the two things the product can act on — a value that is wrong, a thing that should be different — with no rating, no e-mail field, and nothing leaving the device;
    • it says out loud that filing needs a GitHub account (see the sibling issue), because a link that ends at a login screen is a trap rather than a door;
    • it costs no tap target on a 390 px screen that the results already need.

    Not decided: whether the answer is a prompt at all. A quieter option is a line in the results screen's own body, in the page's voice, where the judgement happens — no furniture, no timing, no dismissal state.

    #7 [design-flaw] Every way back in needs a GitHub accountclosed

    Every way back into this product ends at github.com, and filing there needs an account.

    Every way back into this product ends at github.com, and filing there needs an account.

    The tracker link on every page, the "Suggest a route or a change" link, and the route pages' "Report a wrong value" all open github.com/OytunOnal/permit-rulebook-data/issues/new/choose. A reader who is not a developer — which is most of the people this product is for, someone weighing a job offer with a non-EU passport — meets a sign-up wall at exactly the moment they were willing to help.

    Why it is still like this. The product has no backend by design: answers never leave the device, and a test asserts it. A form that posts a report needs somewhere to post it, and that is a server. So the cheapest door was the one that already existed.

    What the options cost, as far as they are understood today:

    • A `mailto:` link — no server, no account, works everywhere. Costs a public address that will be scraped, and a report that lands in a mailbox instead of a tracker with labels and history.
    • A hosted form (a third-party service) — no account for the reader, but a third party now receives what a reader types, which is a promise this product has not made and would have to make carefully; it also adds a connect-src/form-action exception to a policy that currently allows neither.
    • A tiny endpoint of our own — full control, and the first server this project has ever run: a deployment, a secret, an abuse surface, and a sentence in the privacy statement that today reads "there is no backend".
    • Leave it, and say so plainly — keep GitHub, but name the requirement at every door rather than letting a reader discover it after the click.

    Blocks nothing, but it caps everything: the feedback prompt in the sibling issue is worth little if the door behind it is locked.

    Found by the product's own author on 2026-09-15, walking /data/: "footer da eski value'lar… Newest value read 2026-09-10 — bunlar dinamik değil mi?"

The daily watch confirms every source unchanged and, by design, does not move a value's date when it does: the date beside a value is the day that reading was taken, and moving it on every confirmation would erase "unchanged since X".     #11 [design-flaw] Dataset JSON-LD: spatialCoverage as {"@type":"Country"} is "invalid object type" to Googleclosed

    Source: Google Search Console → Datasets, export of 2026-09-16 (Non-critical issues.csv): Invalid object type for field "spatialCoverage", 1 item, validation not started. The dataset itself is valid (1 valid, 0 invalid, every day since 2026-09-11 — the first day Google saw /data/).

    Source: Google Search Console → Datasets, export of 2026-09-16 (Non-critical issues.csv): Invalid object type for field "spatialCoverage", 1 item, validation not started. The dataset itself is valid (1 valid, 0 invalid, every day since 2026-09-11 — the first day Google saw /data/).

    What we send (src/lib/dataset-ld.ts, live on /data/):

    "spatialCoverage": [{"@type":"Country","name":"Germany"}, …]

    Why it is flagged. In schema.org Country is a subtype of Place, so the markup is correct vocabulary — but Google's Dataset parser accepts spatialCoverage only as Text or `{"@type":"Place"}` (its three documented examples: a Place with GeoCoordinates, a Place with GeoShape, or a plain string such as "Tahoe City, CA"). Country is not on its list, hence "invalid object type". Non-critical: the dataset is still indexed and eligible; the property is simply dropped.

    Fix (one line, no copy change): emit the countries as text — spatialCoverage: countries (an array of the four names, or one joined string if the parser wants a single value) — or as {"@type":"Place","name":…}. The names come from dataset.countries, as today. tests/ has the JSON-LD case to extend: assert the type is text/Place, never Country.

    Verify: after the deploy, Search Console → Datasets → the issue's Validate fix; the Rich Results test on https://permitrulebook.com/data/ shows no warning on spatialCoverage.

    Not s13 (in flight); a slice of its own, small, or folded into site #10's /data/ pass.

ARCHITECTURE — Permit Rulebook (as of v1, public and announced 2026-09-09)

Updated at every slice exit. Three diagrams, one question each: where the facts come from, how they become a site, where the reader's answers stay. Labels are names; what each name means is in the list below the diagrams.

1 · Data flow — where the facts come from

flowchart LR
    Sources -->|fetch, hash| Watch --> Flags
    Flags -.->|a person edits| Dataset
    Dataset --> Gates
    Exclusions --> Gates

2 · Pipeline — how the facts become a site

flowchart LR
    Lock -->|which data commit| Build
    Dataset --> Build
    Engine --> Build
    Tokens --> Build
    Build --> Dist --> CI --> Pages
    Watch -->|dataset-updated| CI

3 · Trust boundary — where the answers stay

flowchart LR
    subgraph web["permitrulebook.com"]
        Pages
    end
    subgraph device["The reader's device"]
        RoutePage["Route page"]
        Interview
        Record
    end
    Pages --> RoutePage
    Pages --> Interview
    RoutePage -->|"check yours"| Interview
    Interview --> Record
    Interview -.->|"report a wrong value"| Tracker

Nothing crosses the boundary from the device outward except a reader's own click on the tracker link. Answers never leave the device.

The names

Diagram 1 — permit-rulebook-data (sibling repository; data CC BY 4.0, code MIT)

  • Sources — the official pages and PDFs: ZAV, BOE, UGE, IND, service-public.gouv.fr, europa.eu, efta.int, buzer.
  • Watch — the daily re-read (src/watch, GitHub Actions): strategies html, pdf-text (embedded-font text layer, bytes hash beside words hash) and link liveness; slices bind each page to its operative text; glyph corrections are declared data; human_tier: 2 since 2026-09-10 (two sources a browser reads and this fetcher cannot — see the glossary); runs daily in GitHub Actions and files an issue (source-change) per flag.
  • Flags — one file per change with the quoted diff; read by a person, who edits the dataset or records a false alarm.
  • Dataset — data/dataset.json (schema 0.5.0): 23 routes, 4 countries; every numeric value with its source URL, verbatim quote, retrieval date and append-only history; per route its statements (preconditions and caveats in the source's words), its readings (ours, declared) and a scope value with the limbs it does not ask; the experience ladder is ordinal. Beside it data/countries.json (199 passport issuers, labels, aliases, articles; classes.eu_eea_ch is a rule with four sourced legs) and schema/ruleset.schema.json, the public contract.
  • Exclusions — data/exclusions.md: what was researched and left out, by route, as prose plus a fenced machine twin.
  • Gates — npm run check: schema and semantic validation · watch coverage both ways · quote fidelity (every shipped quote found in its snapshot) · prose provenance (every renderable sentence is authority, ours or label, keyed on the declared kind). A failing gate fails the build downstream.

Diagram 2 — permit-rulebook (this repository; MIT)

  • Engine — pure functions in the data package: eligibility by eq/in/gte/points/any, band edges = thresholds, deciding path, still-reachable, points as the best row an answer satisfies, questions by information gain, provable single-step unlocks, notices; property tests over thousands of generated profiles.
  • Tokens — tokens.css (the design system) and identity.css (the identity pair: the tilted PR stamp over the read-date stamp; one source, inlined by the route page, linked by the interview).
  • Build — Astro, base derived from SITE_URL; an invalid dataset breaks the build; the social card is rendered from the dataset's own facts with a sidecar that makes a stale card fail CI.
  • Dist — 25 pages (the interview, the status page, 23 route pages generated from the dataset, never typed) plus 23 JSON endpoints, favicons, the social card.
  • CI — pages.yml: both repositories checked out, data gates, build, tests with a real browser over dist/, asset truth, base-path check, tap targets at 390 px, then deploy.
  • Pages — GitHub Pages at the custom domain, HTTPS enforced; static files only; the accepted cost is no response headers of our own.

Diagram 3 — the reader

  • Route page — rules in interview order, the rail with its label list, quotes with language and read date, the scope block, the data door (JSON, repository, tracker), one call to action that pre-scopes the interview.
  • Interview — questions by information gain, then results: grouped verdicts, the rail, statements, unlock steps, learn links; runs entirely in the browser.
  • Record — localStorage (legacy key migrated) or nothing; a print stylesheet for a paper copy.
  • Tracker — permit-rulebook-data/issues with bug, design-flaw, new-need; reached only by the reader's own click.
