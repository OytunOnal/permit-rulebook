## Findings

One blocker, eleven friction, nine polish.

### Blocker

**B1 — The stamp's date is unscoped, and on the page whose source did not
answer the daily run the page says nothing.** *`w2-phone-17-route.png` (stamp
"RULES READ 2026-09-10" on the Dutch route page), `w1-desk-24-route.png` (stamp
"2026-10-21" on the German one), `w1-desk-29-data.png` ("A Dutch source did not
answer on the last run; the values it backs were read on 2026-09-10").* Every
page carries the stamp "RULES READ <date>". On `/`, `/data/`, `/feedback/`, the
404 and the German route page it reads 2026-10-21; on the Dutch highly-skilled
route page it reads 2026-09-10. The date is the newest read on that page — the
page never says so, and the stamp's words ("rules read") say something else. The
same route page says, in its own prose, "a daily check re-reads every source",
over a stamp six weeks old, under a footer that says "re-read daily (last run
2026-10-22 · 1 source unread)" without naming the source. Persona B, on the one
route she has, reads: these rules were last read in September; the daily
re-read does not reach this page; one source is unread and it may be this one.
Only `/data/` resolves it, and nothing on the route page points there for this.
**Why a blocker and not friction:** this milestone's own promise is "every date
says what it dates"; the stamp is the largest date on every page and the one
left undefined, and the one page where the gap is real (the unread source) is
the page that carries the oldest stamp with no notice. The previous run filed
the same date as polish (its P1) when its consequence was cosmetic; now the
consequence is a reader doubting the liveness promise on exactly the page where
the promise is strained. **A pass looks like:** the stamp's label names its
scope ("NEWEST VALUE ON THIS PAGE READ 2026-09-10", or the stamp keeps the site
date and the page's own date sits in the panel beneath it, scoped); the page
whose source did not answer says so where its date is ("its source did not
answer on 2026-10-22; the values stand as read on 2026-09-10"); the footer's
"1 source unread" names the page or links to the sentence on `/data/`.

**Ruled by the human, 2026-10-23:** v1.2.2 ships with B1, its reason named in the release's notes. B1 is its own issue, #185, related to #85.

- **Steps look like steps; counters look like counters.** Step headings in
  serif ink, "would be met" in green; "1 OPEN · 3 WITHIN REACH · 4 NOT YET" as
  plain coloured text, not pills.
- **The mirror is named.** "German, from buzer.de" and "Consolidated text on
  buzer.de" where the mirror is the source; "Official page" on the cards points

- **390.** MENU folds the header and pushes the page down; `You declared` folds

## Passes

**Label pass** (each option read in the persona's voice). Hesitations: "No
completed qualification" for a reader with a school-leaving certificate (he has
completed something); "Not assessed yet" against "I don't know" — persona A
chose the first and was right to, but the pair invites a second look;
"Doesn't apply to me, or I don't know" on funds is two answers in one row and
the verdict reads it as the second ("an open unknown"); "Any of these four —
show me everything" wraps to three lines in the declared panel. Nothing demeans.

**Prose pass** (every string of the walk read as a stranger, away from the
screen). Source-language residue: the verbatim quotes, by stated policy (F5);
route names carry the authority's term in brackets, which reads as intended
("kennismigrant", "zoekjaar", "Chancenkarte"). Modelling vocabulary: "the
checker" for the interview on every country and route page ("The checker asks
about 8", "what the checker asks, and what it does not") — plain enough, and
used consistently; "on the record" / "Germany is on the record" (P1). Legal
citations in a question's subtitle (F9). Unexplained: "1 source unread" (P6);
"RULES READ" with no scope (B1). Grammar: "Needs where you studied", "Needs an
age of 30 or older; and at least …" (P1). Numbers: consistent this run — the
hero and the rail print the same form ("up to €1,585/month", "€45,934.20/year or
more").

**Screenshot pass** (every screenshot opened as an image with `tokens.css`
beside it). Off-palette: nothing. Wrong case: nothing. Style collision: the
correction caption in the counter's register (F10). Broken layout: at 390 the
route page's headline panel (P2); at 1280 the feedback buttons (P3). Rendering
defect: none on the rail this run — the dot and the bar both drew. The focus
ring on the verdict's `h1` draws a 576×84 box around two lines of serif
(`w3-desk-06-results.png`): visible and correct, a little heavy.
