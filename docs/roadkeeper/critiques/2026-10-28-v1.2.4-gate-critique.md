# Product critique: v1.2.4 gate (2026-10-28)

**Rubric:** PRODUCT_RUBRIC 1.3 · **Mode:** full walk, isolated run (`product-critique-agent`, a session that did none of the work scored) · **Site:** https://permitrulebook.com, live, dataset 2026-10-21, sources last re-read 2026-10-28 · **Driven by:** the repository's harness (`scripts/browser/browser.mjs`, `walkSite` at `DESK` 1280×900 and `PHONE` 390×844, a fresh profile for each walk) · **Screenshots and scripts:** `.run/critiques/2026-10-28-v1.2.4-gate/`

The walk was blind. Previous critiques, closed issues and the git log stayed closed until the scores below were written. Only then was `2026-10-26-v1.2.3-gate-critique.md` opened to state the delta.

v1.2.4 was an internal milestone (the browser harness and the test server). No screen was meant to change, so any screen that did change counts as a regression.

## Personas walked

The one-pager names one user: a knowledge worker from outside the EU, looking at DE/FR/ES/NL, who has an offer or is about to get one. That user was walked as two people with different stakes, plus the hostile walk:

- **Deniz (desk):** Turkish software engineer with a Berlin offer at about €62,000, a CS degree he has checked in Anabin, six years' experience, English C1 and no German. He is the "first user" profile, and he is the user the TR-agreement data was written for.
- **Priya (phone):** Indian data analyst, 31, with an offer about to land in the Netherlands. She chose "any of these four" because she is still weighing options. She has a master's degree and does not know where German recognition stands. She earns about €4,500/month and has English C1 and no other language.
- **Hostile (desk and phone):** a reflex double-click, "None of these yet", an unknown-heavy profile, a nonsense passport, an EU passport, a former country name, the question's own Back and the browser's Back and Forward, a reload mid-interview, a change of destination mid-interview, Start over, and a 404.

## Findings

No **blocker**. No page is an orphan, nothing lost the user, and no screen put a wrong answer on the record.

### Friction

1. **Interview: half of Priya's questions are about a permit she did not ask about.** She said her offer is in the Netherlands (Q3). Then 9 of her 18 questions served only Germany's Opportunity Card, a permit to come and *look* for work: German recognition, experience in the last five years, experience in the last seven, the German shortage list, German level, English level, a six-month stay in Germany, a partner's card, and blocked-account money (Q7–Q10 and Q14–Q18, each with "Asked for: Opportunity Card (Chancenkarte, § 20a) · Germany"). The "Asked for" line is honest. The cost still falls on a phone user who has an offer elsewhere. Deniz, with an offer and four routes already open, also answered the blocked-account question for the same card (his Q12). User cost: the longest stretch of the interview reads as irrelevant, and on a phone that is where people quit. *Screens:* `20-p2-phone-q07.png` … `20-p2-phone-q18.png`, `10-p1-desk-q12.png`.
2. **Interview: an answer that changes nothing.** "Related skilled work experience in the last seven years?" is put to Deniz under "Asked for: EU Blue Card — shortage occupation". He has a fully recognised degree. His result with "Under 3 years" is line-for-line identical to his result with "5 years or more" (176 lines each, none differs). The answer only bears on the no-degree IT path, which is not his. Either the question goes for a recognised-degree holder, or a rule is missing. *Screens:* `10-p1-desk-q07.png`; the comparison is `w13-p1-variant.mjs` against `10-p1-desk-result-text.txt`.
3. **Results: "Within reach" names routes that are far away, and names them unevenly.** With a salary of "under €33,085.09", both German Blue Cards sit under **WITHIN REACH**. That is at least €12,849/year short of the shortage amount (€45,934.20) and €17,615 short of the general one (€50,700). Read in the user's voice, "within reach" says "nearly there". For Priya, the NL Blue Card is "within reach" at up to €1,585/month short. The highly-skilled-migrant permit, with the *same* €5,942 threshold and the same shortfall, sits under "Not yet" because it also carries an open unknown. Two routes in the same position get two different words. *Screens:* `03-disc-result-full.png`, `20-p2-phone-result-s2.png`, `20-p2-phone-result-s5.png`.
4. **Results: four routes open, and no word on where to start.** Deniz gets four "Criteria met" cards: the Blue Card for shortage occupations, the general Blue Card, the skilled worker with an academic degree (§ 18b) and the experienced worker (§ 19c). The page does not compare them or name a first step. The only next action on each card is "Official page ↗", and the general Blue Card and § 18b point to different BAMF pages. A layperson cannot tell which one to apply for, or whether the choice matters. *Screens:* `10-p1-desk-result-fold.png`, `11-p1-result-s1.png` … `s4`.
5. **Trust: the evidence for every German value is in German.** Every DE quote on the results and route pages is the authority's German sentence ("Personen mit einem Mangelberuf und Berufseinsteigende können die Blaue Karte EU auch mit einem geringeren Bruttojahresgehalt beantragen…"). The site's interface is English, and so are its users (one-pager: "Interface EN"). The data page explains why it never translates. The cards say nothing about what the sentence says, so the differentiator ("this number is from that page, with that quote") lands only for readers of German. Deniz trusts the number because of the date and the link, not because he could read the quote. *Screens:* `11-p1-route-s3.png`, `11-p1-route-s4.png`, `10-p1-desk-result-fold.png`.
6. **Copy: modelling vocabulary on the country pages, the route pages and the result cards.** The country pages show "quoted and dated · the checker asks about it, except 1 condition, in our own words, not the authority's" under every route, and route pages and cards repeat it ("On this route — quoted and dated · the checker asks about it, except 4 conditions"). This is the coverage tier, written in internal vocabulary, and a stranger cannot parse it. The edit-mode banner "YOUR ANSWER — PICK ANOTHER TO CHANGE IT; ANSWERS YOUR NEW CHOICE STILL USES ARE KEPT" has the same problem. *Screens:* `12-desk-france-s1.png`, `42-germany-s1.png`, `12-phone-france-s1.png`, `11-p1-edit-salary.png`.

## Passes

**Label pass.** Read in the user's voice, the options do not demean anyone. "None of these yet — exploring my options" and "I don't know" are offered everywhere they matter. Three hesitations:

- "Within reach" (finding 3).
- "Doesn't apply to me, or I don't know" on the salary question of someone who has just said "I have a job offer". It merges two very different answers, and the offer holder wonders which one they are giving.
- "Assessed and not recognised" is accurate and painful, but there is no softer honest word.

**Prose pass.** All strings the walks produced are in the `*-text.txt` files. Read end to end away from the screen:

- **Source-language residue:** the German quotes (finding 5); "Chancenkarte", "BeschV", "AufenthG", "Anmeldung", "kennismigrant", "zoekjaar", "salarié détaché", "tarjeta azul-UE" in names. Most of these are glossed in parentheses, and the § citations are not.
- **Modelling vocabulary:** "quoted and dated · the checker asks about it" (finding 6); "an open gap, not a no", which works; "unlocking steps"; "record generated" on the stamp.
- **Abbreviations:** CEFR, ISCO-08, MVV, BIG and IND are expanded where they appear. § is not.

**Screenshot pass.** Every screenshot was opened beside `tokens.css`. No colour sits off the palette, and the measured contrast of every visible text element on home, Germany, a route page, data, feedback, 404, the results page (desk) and the unknown-heavy results page (phone) passes AA (`41-contrast.txt`; none below 4.5:1). The defects are the ones listed above: the read date at display size (9), the masthead shift (10), the link and hover colours (13, 14), the search field's width (15) and the focus ring's overlap (16). The phone layout never scrolls sideways (`scrollWidth` 390 on the longest results page).

## Adoption verdicts

- **Deniz:** finishes in 12 questions, comes back when his salary or job changes, and recommends it. The verdict is clear and every number is dated. **Most likely to stop him:** four open routes and no hint which one to file, with the decisive quotes in German.
- **Priya:** finishes on her phone in 18 questions, and the result is honest: nothing open, the NL Blue Card short by up to €1,585/month, and the IND's designated-institution list one tap away as "an open gap, not a no". She would come back after checking that list, and would recommend it with a caveat. **Most likely to stop her:** the nine German job-search questions that follow her answer "Netherlands".
- **Hostile:** could not break it. The double-click was absorbed (still on Q2), a nonsense passport gets "No country matches. Try fewer letters…", an EU passport gets a designed "No work permit needed." with three quotes, "Burma" finds Myanmar, Back/Forward/reload keep every answer, and a destination change keeps the answers still in use. **Most likely to stop them:** nothing on the screen. The risk is a false "within reach" sending someone after a route they are far from.

## Recommended adjustments (prioritized, for the human's pick)

1. **Ask only what can change this user's answer** (findings 1, 2; carried #189, #186). Once the offer country is known, put the other countries' job-search permit (Germany's Opportunity Card) behind one opt-in card ("Also check Germany's permit to come and look for work?"), not nine questions in the middle. Skip the seven-year experience question when a recognised degree has already settled the shortage Blue Card. Skip the blocked-account question when an offer route is already met. This cuts Priya's 18 questions to about 9 and Deniz's 12 to about 10, closer to the one-pager's 6–7 (A10).
2. **Make the result's words match the distance, and give a first step** (findings 3, 4). Keep "within reach" for a shortfall the reader could plausibly close (or rename the tier to what it is, "one condition short"), and give two routes in the same position the same tier. When more than one route is open, add one line saying how they differ and which one the authority treats as the usual door, as an "Our reading" line the site already knows how to mark.
3. **Make the evidence readable and the coverage line plain** (findings 5, 6). Keep each German or French quote as the record. Under it, add a one-line English gist marked as ours, in the same device as "Our reading, not the authority's words". Replace "quoted and dated · the checker asks about it, except 1 condition, in our own words, not the authority's" with a sentence a stranger can read ("We ask about all of this except one condition, listed below").

The other findings (7–20) become issues through `road`, labelled as marked and folded as listed above.
