## Findings

Two blockers, nine friction, nine polish. Screenshots in
`docs/spine/critiques/2026-10-18-v1.2-gate-critique/`.

### Blockers

**B1 — On the Germany job-offer path, "And in the last seven years?" is asked
and no card on the result reads the answer.** *`06-p1-q8-seven-years-desktop.jpg`,
`09-p1-results-top-desktop.jpg`, `10-p1-results-steps-desktop.jpg`,
`11-p1-results-notyet-bluecard-desktop.jpg`.* Priya declares "3 to under 5
years" in the last seven. The eight Germany cards and the "Steps that would
unlock more" block mention the five-year answer (Experienced worker; the
Opportunity Card's "+2"), the recognition answer, the salary, the funds — and
never the seven-year one; a text search of the whole results screen for
seven/sieben/7 years finds nothing. The rubric's own rule: an answer that
changes nothing the user sees means either the question goes or a rule is
missing. On the hostile walk the same answer *is* read — by Spain ("EU Blue Card
— needs a university degree or 5+ years of related experience in the last
seven", `45-hostile-results-any-spain.jpg`) — so the question exists for other
countries and is asked of the German reader for nothing. **Why a blocker and not
friction:** Priya is the one-pager's first persona, and her Blue Card cards both
say "not yet — an open gap" on recognition alone. If the seven-year experience
can open a German Blue Card for her occupation (which only the human can
confirm from the sources), the core persona is told "not yet" on her best route;
if it cannot, she has been asked a question that is noise on the path she took.
Either way the first user leaves with less trust than she arrived with.
**A pass looks like:** on the Germany job-offer path, the seven-year answer
appears on at least one card's ledger (met, short, or "would be met with…"), or
the question is not asked of a Germany-only reader.

**B2 — The route page's headline panel dates a value differently from the
sentence it says it came from.** *`13-route-experienced-worker-top-desktop.jpg`
against `14-route-experienced-worker-rail-desktop.jpg`.* "WHAT THIS ROUTE ASKS
FOR — €45,630/year … Read from the authority's own page on **2026-09-17**, with
the sentence it came from below." The sentence below: "mind. 45.630 Euro im Jahr
2026 · arbeitsagentur.de · read **2026-09-02**". Same number, same page, two
read dates, and the panel's own wording binds them to each other. The masthead
stamp says 09-17; every country-card says "read 2026-09-17"; the footer says
"values read between 2026-09-02 and 2026-09-16 · re-read daily (last run
2026-10-18) · dataset 2026-09-18". The product's one claim is "per value, that
page, that date"; the page built to prove it contradicts itself on the first
value shown. The previous run filed the four-date footer as polish (F14); the
headline-versus-quote pair is sharper and sits on every route page's first
screen. **A pass looks like:** one date per value, or two dates with two verbs
("first read 2026-09-02 · confirmed unchanged 2026-09-17"), and the same pair
used by the stamp, the country cards and the footer.

### Polish

**P1 — Four dates, three verbs, no key, on every page.** *`12-p1-results-bottom-footer-desktop.jpg`,
`18-data-page-desktop.jpg`.* Stamp 09-17; footer "read between 09-02 and 09-16 ·
last run 10-18 · dataset 09-18"; data page "newest value changed 09-16 · last

**P2 — The country page's h1 starts lowercase: "the Netherlands: 6 routes
scored, 1 quoted."** *`32-390-netherlands-country-page.png`.* The article is
part of the country's name string and is not capitalised at the start of a
heading. The same string reads correctly mid-sentence ("in the Netherlands").

**P3 — "there itself" on the all-four path.** *`38-hostile-q2-any-there-itself.jpg`.*
"An employment contract with an employer there itself — including one you
already hold" — the country name was substituted with "there" inside a phrase
built for a name.

**P4 — Modelling vocabulary on every card and in two h1s.** *`17-germany-country-page-desktop.jpg`
("Germany: 8 routes scored, 1 quoted."), `09-p1-results-top-desktop.jpg`
("quoted and dated · scored — 2 conditions this interview did not ask"),
`30-p2-390-results-notyet-card.png` ("scored — 5 conditions this interview did
not ask and 1 condition in our own words, not the authority's").* "Scored",
"conditions", "rule sets", "this interview" are the dataset's words; the
vocational card's trailing "in our own words, not the authority's" reads like a
data flag printed by accident. *(Carried: F7 of 2026-09-23.)*

**P5 — Statutory shorthand bare in route titles and on cards.** "§ 19c / § 6

**P6 — "Not assessed / not recognised" is one option for two different
situations.** *`05-p1-q6-recognition-desktop.jpg`.* Priya's degree has never

**P7 — Salary bands carry two-decimal edges and are the same nine for every

"€45,630 – under €45,934.20". *(Carried: F3 of 2026-09-23.)*

**P8 — The address button confirms only to a screen reader.** *`37-390-feedback-copy-address.png`.*
After the click a polite live region says "Copied"; on screen the address text

**P9 — "Criteria met" has two homes at 390.** *`22-p2-390-arrive-restored-results.png`

## Passes

**Label pass** (each option read in the persona's voice). Hesitations: "Not
assessed / not recognised" (P6); "No completed qualification" is fine for a
graduate but a reader with a three-year degree from a non-listed college may
wonder if "University degree" is hers to tick; "Vocational training, 2+ years
(state-recognised where completed)" asks the reader to know a legal fact at the
moment of ticking; "Over 40" as the last age band with no "why we ask" (the
Opportunity Card points, the Dutch under-30 split) — a 44-year-old with an offer
is asked her age and never told what it changed. Nothing demeans.

**Prose pass** (every string of the walk read as a stranger, away from the
screen). Source-language residue: the verbatim quotes, by stated policy, with
the policy on `/data/` only (the last run's F1, unchanged); route names carry
the authority's term in brackets, which reads as intended ("kennismigrant",
"zoekjaar", "Chancenkarte"). Modelling vocabulary: "scored", "rule sets",
"conditions this interview did not ask", "in our own words, not the authority's"
(P4). Unexplained abbreviations: BeschV, AufenthG, ISCO-08, TVG, IND (glossed
once as "Dutch immigration service (IND)" and then used bare), MVV, IELTS,
BIG, A1–C1 (P5). Grammar: "there itself" (P3); "the Netherlands:" at the head
of a sentence (P2); "And in the last seven years?" with no "last five" before
it (F3).

**Screenshot pass** (every screenshot opened as an image with `tokens.css`
beside it). Off-palette: nothing. Wrong case: P2. Style collision: none seen;
the blue underlined "Read the rules of this route" in `--color-band` is the last
run's F11 and still renders that way. Broken layout: none at 1920 or 390; the
rail captions at 390 wrap into right-aligned fragments as before (the last
run's F15). The 404 keeps the site tagline as its deck (the last run's F13).

## Recommended adjustments

Three, prioritised, in pick-and-apply form. **Apply all**, **apply 1 and 2**,
or **apply 1** — each stands alone.

**1 — Make every asked answer visible in the result, starting with the
seven-year experience on the Germany path.** *(Closes B1; also F2's funds
question and F7's kept answers; lifts lenses 2 and 5.)* Rule: a question is
asked only when at least one route in the chosen scope reads its answer, and
every answer in the declared panel appears on at least one card's ledger — met,
short, or "would be met with…". For Germany that means either a card reads
"3 to under 5 years in the last seven" (if a German rule turns on it — the human
checks the source), or the question is dropped from the Germany-only branch.
For the funds question on an offer path, either say on the question why it is
asked ("for the Opportunity Card, which needs no offer") or ask it only on the
exploring branch; and give the "doesn't apply" row its pencil. After a country
change, drop or grey the answers no route in the new scope reads.

**2 — One date per value, with a verb.** *(Closes B2 and P1; lifts lens 4.)*
Every place a date is printed — the headline panel on a route page, the
country cards, the stamp, the footer — uses the same two-date form: "first read
2026-09-02 · confirmed unchanged 2026-09-17" (or "re-read daily, last
2026-10-18" where that is the fact), and the stamp's label says which of the two
it is. No new data: both dates already exist on the page; the panel's sentence
"Read from the authority's own page on …, with the sentence it came from below"
just has to agree with the sentence below it.

**3 — Give the reader a beat between questions, and the country's own
number first.** *(Closes F4 and F8; hardens the hostile path; lifts lens 2.)*
After an answer, move keyboard-and-mouse focus to the new question's heading
(not its first option) and let the new card arrive with a short, token-timed
reveal, so a second click in the same spot lands on nothing; and on country
cards and route headline panels, lead with the amount that applies to the
largest group ("from €5,942/month · €3,122 for recent graduates"), keeping the
rail as it is.
